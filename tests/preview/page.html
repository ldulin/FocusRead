Sentence segmentation under adversarial punctuation

Prior work (Smith et al., 2020) reported an effect at p < 0.05 across three cohorts. We replicated this in a pre-registered sample. See Fig. 3 for the full distribution, and cf. Jones for a contrasting account.

Participants (N = 24, mean age 22.4 yrs) completed 3 runs of the task. Each run took 6.5 min. Data are available at https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4499 for reuse. The dataset is open access.

1. Methods

We used a Butterworth filter, i.e. a maximally flat response, before resampling to 1 kHz. The pipeline was implemented by J. R. Smith and validated against the U.S. National Institute of Standards reference set. Stimuli included images, sounds, etc. Results were consistent across modalities.

Although the effect was small in the first cohort, we observed a consistent increase across all three replication samples, which suggests that the underlying mechanism is robust to variation in both the stimulus set and the participant population.
